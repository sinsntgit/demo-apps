The idea

What is an ontology?

A formal, shared model of a domain, written so that people and machines read it the same way.

Why it matters

What an ontology gives you

1

One shared meaning

Every team and every system uses the same definition of each business term.

2

Answers you can trust

The same question returns the same answer, whoever asks, in business language.

3

Connected data

Systems link through the shared model instead of one-off mappings between every pair.

4

AI on solid ground

Models answer from your definitions and your data, instead of guessing.

Without one

Three systems, three meanings

Sales

“Customer” = someone who placed an order

Support

“Customer” = anyone with an account

Finance

“Customer” = the entity we invoice
“How many customers do we have?”
12,400Sales
18,950Support

The impact

What going without costs

  • ⚖
    Conflicting reportsMeetings spent arguing about whose number is right.
  • 🔗
    Fragile, costly integrationsEvery new system mapped to every other, one pair at a time.
  • 🧠
    Knowledge locked in people’s headsNew joiners and auditors cannot see why a number means what it does.
  • 🤖
    AI that guessesNo agreed definitions to ground it: confident answers nobody can verify.
  • 📋
    Compliance riskNo clear line from a rule or regulatory term to the data that answers it.
