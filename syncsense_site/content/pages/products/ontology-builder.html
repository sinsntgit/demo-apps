Product · Ontology & knowledge graph

Ontology Studio: one shared model of your business data

The Ontology Studio creates a connected model of what your business terms mean and how they relate, then fills it with your records and documents as a knowledge graph. Reports, analytics and AI assistants then use the same definitions, and AI answers can cite where each fact came from. It is for data leaders whose definitions differ between teams, and for AI projects that need answers they can trust.

Who it is for

  • Chief data officers and data governance teams who need one definition of core business terms.
  • Teams building AI assistants that must answer from company knowledge, with citations.
  • Regulated businesses that need lineage from a reported figure back to its source.
  • Organisations where the answer to one question sits across several systems.

Problems it solves

  • “Customer”, “revenue” or “policy” mean different things in different systems.
  • Plain search misses relationships: a ruling that was overruled, a charge caused by an outage.
  • Generic AI tools invent sources or mix up terms.
  • AI pilots stall because nobody wrote down what the data means.

How does the Ontology Studio work?

  1. Collect sources

    Documents, databases and system feeds: for example Acts and case law for a tax firm, or billing and network systems for a telecom operator.

  2. Define the ontology

    Classes, relations and properties for the domain. Our tax ontology has 165 classes, 108 relations and 74 properties.

  3. Build the knowledge graph

    Records and passages are loaded into Neo4j with vector embeddings, tagged with metadata such as source, date and status.

  4. Answer with evidence

    Queries follow the graph, citations and meaning together, so AI answers cite the document, page or event behind each claim.

Start from a pre-built library

Ontology Studio includes 22 ready-made ontologies across 19 industries, from banking, insurance and telecommunications to healthcare, retail, manufacturing and the public sector. Each one lists the standards it builds on, such as FIBO for financial services, HL7 FHIR and SNOMED CT for healthcare, and Schema.org, along with its classes, properties and use cases.

Open one to look at it, copy it into your own library, and extend it for your business.

Key features

Diagram and model editor

See the class hierarchy, one module’s relationships, or everything around one class. Add classes, relationships and attributes from the diagram.

Test bed and quality checks

Try the ontology against your use cases before relying on it, and check quality and reasoning results in one place.

Versions and sharing

Every change is saved as a new version you can go back to. Share an ontology with everyone or keep it private.

Domain understanding

Defines what each business object is: a section, a holding and a circular in tax; a subscriber, a plan and a usage record in telecom.

Relationship understanding

Captures how things relate across systems: overruled, superseded, caused by, billed for, covered by.

Metadata, not guesswork

Forum, binding reach, effective dates and source are stored as metadata, so the AI does not have to guess them.

GraphRAG retrieval

Knowledge graph and vector search together, with several retrieval modes, so answers use connected evidence.

Safe queries at scale

AI-generated queries are checked against the schema before they run; one query can scan all customers instead of one.

Lineage and governance

Each fact keeps a link to its source system or document, which supports audits and regulatory reporting.
